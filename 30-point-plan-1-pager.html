Restoring ICCSD's Financial Footing

One-page summary of the 30-point plan dated June 9, 2026

You already have the full 30-point plan. This is the one-page version so we don't have to thumb through nine pages over coffee.

The plan groups 30 actions into nine areas that smaller districts run every year. Most can begin within 30 days. Almost none require new money. This is constructive input from a community member, not a critique of staff or the board.

Items already in motion based on public communications are marked. Others may also be in progress, and I just don't have visibility into them yet.

1. Get to a monthly rhythm. Items 1–4

Close the books inside 10 business days of period end, with variance commentary on a fixed calendar. Reconcile every bank, investment, and ledger account monthly with a named signer. Produce a monthly cash position. Track compliance with the new procedures on a sustained scorecard.

In motion
Monthly close rolls out July 1.

2. Forecast the year, then report against it. Items 5–7

Approve a full-year budget broken out by month. Reforecast every month using actuals to date. Report actuals against both budget and forecast, with written commentary on any line that moves past a set threshold.

3. Targets for the metrics that matter. Items 8–11

A short list of financial health metrics with numeric targets, benchmarked against comparable Iowa districts. Days of cash, solvency ratio, unspent authorized budget ratio. Assign each target to a named leader. Define recovery as sustaining the targets for a full fiscal year, and say so publicly.

4. Current on audits and required filings. Items 12–15

Lock and publish the full audit schedule through FY26. Add capacity before a deadline is missed, not after. File every overdue state report and confirm receipt in writing. Report audit progress publicly at every board meeting until the district is current.

In motion
FY24 final report targeted for the June 23 board meeting.

5. Deal with the credit rating directly. Items 16–18

Request a meeting with the rating agency and publish the path they lay out for restoring the rating. Disclose all outstanding debt, terms, and covenants in one public place. Confirm in writing whether any covenant or continuing-disclosure obligation was missed.

6. Make finances genuinely transparent. Items 19–21

Adopt a public financial data repository as the official home, updated on a fixed schedule. Post the monthly close package publicly within 5 business days of the board receiving it. Publish a plain-language summary each month that a parent can read in a few minutes.

In motion
A free public repository already exists. The monthly statement format is being designed.

7. Fix the board's own oversight. Items 22–25

A standing audit and finance committee meeting monthly under a published charter. A fixed written list of items the board sees every month. An independent financial voice advising the board, separate from staff. A written escalation rule for missed closes, missed filings, or control failures.

Where the FOC fits

8. Leadership and accountability. Items 26–28

A single accountable leader with a written scope of responsibility. A clean leadership transition so incoming leadership has the room to fix this. An independent after-action review of how this happened. The point of the review is prevention, not blame.

9. Make sure it does not happen again. Items 29–30

Document the basic financial controls in writing and test them once a year. Report against this plan in public, action by action, until every item is complete.

If you would prefer I retire any of these items, I would rather hear it from you than guess. The plan is a starting point, not a list of demands.
