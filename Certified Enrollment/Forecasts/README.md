# Iowa City Schools Enrollment Forecast Analysis

An independent review of how ICCSD enrollment forecasts compared with actual enrollment, why they differed, and what it means going forward. Built to support sound planning, not to assign blame.

## What is in this folder

- **ICCSD_Enrollment_Story.html** - the narrative report. Open in any browser and scroll. Self-contained, works offline.
- **ICCSD_Enrollment_Forecast_vs_Actual.xlsx** - the workbook behind the report. Seven tabs: district forecasts vs actual, the state benchmark, demographic drivers, the bond timeline, the official-statement projections, an overshoot table, and sources.
- **Sources/** - copies of every raw input used. Originals were left in place elsewhere in the ICCSD folder.

## Sources

- **District demographer reports** - DeJong-Richter (2013, 2015, 2016), Granville (2023), Cooperative Strategies / Woolpert (2025), plus the facilities capacity sheet and the FY24 budget enrollment forecast.
- **State of Iowa data** - Iowa Department of Education certified enrollment and certified-enrollment projections by district, plus county birth rates, K-12 immigrant counts, and K-12 migrant counts. Iowa City is district 3141.
- **GO bond official statements** - the Series 2017C, 2019, and 2020 statements filed with bond investors (MSRB EMMA).
- **Bond and audited financials** - the FY22 audited financial statements (bond issuance dates and amounts), the GO bond enrollment marketing sheet, and the 2017 ten-year financial projection.

## The short version

- The 2015 and 2016 forecasts pointed toward roughly 16,000 students, and a $191.5M bond built schools for that growth.
- Actual enrollment leveled off near 14,400. The State of Iowa's independent projections were consistently more conservative and closer to reality.
- The cause was demographic. Local births peaked in 2016, and rising immigration cushioned the slowdown.
- The newest forecast is the first to project a decline, toward about 13,600 by 2034-35. Funding follows enrollment in Iowa, so budget pressure has arrived, including $7.5M in cuts for 2026-27.
- The path forward is to plan in ranges, watch independent benchmarks, and refresh the forecast often.

## A note on counting methods

Enrollment is measured several ways (certified or resident, building headcount, and total served) that differ by a few hundred students. Forecasts and actuals are shown on their own published bases. The Chapter 4 comparison rebases both 2015 forecasts to a common starting point so they compare on equal footing.
