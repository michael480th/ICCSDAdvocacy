Committee commentary — FY24 audit findings The FY2024 audit was issued June 10, 2026 — roughly two years after the June 30, 2024 year-end — and carries eight material weaknesses, with the late filing itself cited as an instance of noncompliance (2024-006). The most severe cluster is around segregation of duties, cash reconciliation, and year-end fund adjustment (2024-001 through 005), which the auditor links to business-office turnover. Recommended committee action: require the CFO to report against this tracker at each FOC meeting, hold the FY25-filed-by-November-2026 and FY26-filed-by-March-2027 commitments (2024-006) as hard deadlines, and request documented closure evidence for each material weakness before it is marked closed.
Other control deficiencies (auditor management letter, below significant-deficiency level). The auditor separately identified lower-level recommendations the committee should track to completion:
  • Disbursements — 9 of 25 sampled disbursements lacked a purchase order; 4 not on the Board bill listing; travel pre-approvals and credit-card receipts missing; ESSER coding errors.
  • Payroll — no HR-to-payroll contract reconciliation; I-9s held in payroll; unsigned contracts processed after pay; an FY23 rate used for FY24 overtime; lunch-break export discrepancy.
  • Receipts — nutrition price/POS reconciliation gaps (variances $202–$41,076); athletics gate receipts paid by check to ADs; admin-building receipt segregation.
  • Journal entries / Technology — no formal JE approval; GL software administrator should move to IT; underutilized, non-integrated software.
  • Stale-dated checks & capital assets — outstanding checks from FY2004–FY2023; manual Excel asset/depreciation tracking; capitalization-threshold policy update (GASB 87/96).
  • Child Nutrition & Early Retirement — non-public-purpose meeting food; undocumented meal-claim adjustments; early-retirement packages exceeding Board policy by $870,324 (2023) and $389,019 (2024) without Board approval.
