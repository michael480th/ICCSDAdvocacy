ICCSD · FY24 Budget — Resolved

How the FY24 budget question came out

Before the audit landed, this page asked one question: would the FY24 audit show Iowa City Community School District exceeding its legal budget for a second straight year? The audit, with a report dated June 10, 2026, has now answered it. The short version: the audit confirms a certified-budget violation — but in capital spending, not in the General Fund cushion this page had focused on. This page has been updated to report what the audit actually found.

The answer, in one paragraph

The FY24 audit reports that the district overexpended the "other expenditures" function by $19,502,993 — a violation of Iowa's certified-budget law (Chapter 24), listed as audit finding IV-A-24. That overage is in the capital-heavy "other" function, not in instruction or general operations. Separately, the audit reports no finding that the General Fund exceeded its school-finance-formula spending authority in FY24 — the specific measure that was violated in FY23. So FY24 is a budget violation, but not a repeat of the FY23 violation type.

Two different "legal budgets" — and which one broke

Iowa law constrains district spending in two distinct ways, and the FY24 audit treats them separately. Keeping them apart is the key to reading the result.

  • The certified budget (Iowa Code Chapter 24). The board certifies a budget built on four functional categories — instruction, support services, noninstructional programs, and other expenditures — covering all funds combined. The legal control is at the function level. A district may not spend past a function's certified amount without first amending the budget through a public hearing. This is the limit the FY24 audit found violated.
  • General Fund spending authority (Iowa Code Chapter 257). A formula based on enrollment, state aid, and the tax base sets the maximum the General Fund may spend. Exceeding it produces a negative Unspent Authorized Budget (UAB) and brings the district before the School Budget Review Committee. This is the limit the district violated in FY23 (−1.20%). The FY24 audit reports no such finding.

What the audit found: the certified budget

The audit's budgetary comparison schedule lays out the four functions, actual against the amended budget. During the year the district adopted one budget amendment increasing budgeted expenditures by $24,169,846. Even after that amendment, total spending still came in over budget, entirely because of the "other expenditures" function:

The audit's finding IV-A-24 records two items: (1) expenditures exceeded the certified budget in the support function prior to the May 2024 amendment — a procedural violation, since the law requires amending before exceeding, even though full-year support spending finished under the amended figure; and (2) expenditures exceeded the amended certified budget in the other function by the $19.5 million above. Where this page was right, and where it was off

This page was published before the audit, predicting that a thin $3.31 million General Fund cushion (the +1.67% the district had filed) could be erased by unbooked bills. The audit lets us check that prediction honestly.

Where the framing was off: the FY24 violation was not a General Fund cushion erased by a few accrued bills. It was a $19.5 million overage in the district-wide "other" function, which is dominated by capital outlay ($85.3 million of capital spending ran through the year). And the audit reports no General Fund formula-authority violation, so the "two consecutive years of the same violation" scenario — and the escalation reasoning that rode on it — did not come to pass on that measure.

Where the instinct was right: the page identified capital and construction spending as the most likely pressure point, and that is exactly where the budget broke. It also flagged the early-retirement obligation; the audit did make an early-retirement correction, restating beginning net position down by $870,324 — though as a prior-period, government-wide accrual adjustment, not a General Fund budget item. The other two scenarios (federal payroll-tax penalties; an unbooked special-education liability) did not appear as drivers of the FY24 fund results, though the audit did separately fault the district's special-education claims documentation (findings 2024-011 and 2024-014, and a qualified opinion on the Special Education Cluster).

What it means

The certified-budget violation is a statutory finding the district must correct, and it is the kind of compliance item the state tracks. Two points keep it in proportion:

  • It is a real violation, not a paperwork footnote. Iowa law requires amending a function's budget before spending past it. The district amended its budget by $24.2 million during the year and still overran the "other" function by $19.5 million, which means the overspending outpaced even the amended plan.
  • It is a different violation from FY23. FY23 was a General Fund formula-authority breach (−1.20%) — the trigger for School Budget Review Committee oversight. The FY24 audit does not report a repeat of that specific breach, so claims of "two straight years over the spending limit" need to specify which limit: the certified functional budget (yes, FY24) versus General Fund formula authority (FY23, and not reported again for FY24).
