1:1 Conversation Prep

Coffee with Ruthina Malone

Thursday, June 18, 2026  ·  10:30–11:30am CT  ·  Java House, Iowa City  ·  First in-person 1:1
Get to know Ruthina before submitting my FOC application. Have an honest conversation about the committee, the moment, and whether I'd be a good fit. Curiosity over expertise.
1 · Warm up ~5 min
  • Thanks for making time — coffee logistics.
  • How's your summer going so far? Family doing OK with the slower schedule?
  • How's the board year feeling now that you're a few weeks past the May meeting cycle?
2 · Her story — how she got here ~10 min
  • What made you decide to run for the board originally?
  • What's surprised you most about board service vs. what you expected?
  • What's been the most rewarding part of being president?
  • What's been the hardest part of this last year?
3 · Her vision for the district ~10 min
  • When you imagine ICCSD three years from now, what does “working” look like to you?
  • What's something you really want to accomplish before your term ends?
  • Setting finances aside — what's the thing that doesn't get enough attention?
  • What do you think makes ICCSD ICCSD? What's worth protecting?
4 · The FOC — her hopes for it ~15 min
  • How is the financial situation actually showing up in board meetings now — agenda, energy, public comment?
  • What's your hope for the FOC twelve months in? What would tell you it's working?
  • What kind of community members are you hoping show up to apply?
  • What worries you about the committee — what could go sideways?
  • How do you want the FOC to interact with the full board? With staff? With the public?
  • Is there a model from another district or board you're drawing from?
5 · Me — if/when she asks ~5 min
  • Brief on why I'm interested (BLUF, right column).
  • What I'd bring — honest, not a pitch.
  • Invite her questions: “What would you want to know about me?”
6 · Close ~5 min
  • Anyone else you'd suggest I talk to — board, staff, community?
  • What's the best way to stay in touch through the application process?
  • Thank her, specifically. Walk her out.
What I want to remember
  • Be useful, not loud.
  • Curiosity over expertise.
  • Eye contact matters more than notes.
  • Silence is OK — let her think.
  • Honest answers, even when I don't know.
  • Respect what she's lived through this year.
My BLUF — if she asks “why the FOC?”

I have a finance and operations background, two kids in the district, and a year of watching this from the public side. I think the committee is the right structure at the right moment, and I'd like to help make it work.

I want to be useful, not loud. The committee succeeds if the board trusts what comes out of it — and that starts with people who do the homework, respect the staff, and don't make the meeting about themselves.

Filler questions — for a pause
  • What's a board moment you've been most proud of?
  • Who's been the most important mentor for you in this role?
  • If a brand-new board member walked in tomorrow, what would you want them to understand first?
  • What's a question you wish parents asked more often?
  • What do you do to decompress from all of this?
  • What's a misconception people have about being on this board?
  • What do you wish the public understood about how staff have shown up this year?
How I want to close

“I'm planning to submit my FOC application this week. If anything I've shared today raises a flag, I'd rather hear it from you now than learn it later.”

“And if you'd be open to it, I'd love to come back to you with questions as I learn more about the committee.”
