From Fewer Students to Fewer Dollars

Translating the K-12 enrollment forecast into a recurring revenue headwind — and laying it against the district's cash cushion

Why this matters. Iowa funds districts on a per-pupil basis. ICCSD's own cohort-survival forecast projects roughly 750 fewer students by 2030 (Baseline). At the state foundation rate that is a recurring ~$6.8M/year hole in the operating budget — arriving while the district already runs one of the thinnest cash cushions of any large Iowa district.

The bridge, in four steps

Enrollment falls ~752 students by 2030 under the Baseline scenario (14,227 K-12 in 2025 → 13,475 in 2030). The decline is demographic — Johnson County births peaked in 2016 and feed smaller kindergarten classes.
Each lost student removes ~$9,000 of revenue. Iowa's funding formula pays districts per pupil; fewer students means a smaller combined state-aid + property-tax foundation entitlement the following year.
That compounds to a recurring ~$6.8M/year shortfall by 2030 versus the 2025 revenue base — and roughly $18M cumulative (2,027 student-years) of foregone state aid over 2026–2030 as the decline phases in.
It lands on a thin cushion. ICCSD holds about 31 days of net cash (≈$18M in the General Fund) versus a 90-day target (≈$52M). A recurring $6.8M/year drain is roughly a third of the district's entire current cash balance — every year.

Against the cushion

General-fund cash cushion vs. the recurring headwind ($M)

The district is already ~$34M below a 90-day cushion. A recurring revenue decline doesn't just block rebuilding that cushion — left unmatched by spending reductions, it erodes what little remains. This is why the enrollment forecast is a financial document, not just a demographic one.

Bottom line. The ~752-student Baseline decline translates to a recurring ~$6.8M/year revenue headwind by 2030 (range ~$6.1M–$8.3M across scenarios), against a General Fund holding only ~31 days of cash. Closing the gap requires either reversing the enrollment trend (largely demographic, hard to move) or cutting recurring operating costs by a comparable amount. Doing neither spends down a cushion that is already the thinnest among large Iowa districts.

Caveats

  • Iowa's formula uses prior-year certified enrollment with a budget guarantee and a declining- enrollment supplement that soften single-year swings; the figures here are the gross per-pupil impact, not a line-item budget projection.
  • Per-pupil revenue is an all-in approximation; categorical funding that scales with enrollment is included in the $9,000 figure, and a conservative $7,800 alternative is shown.
  • The cushion translation (days → $M) uses the district's own General Fund benchmark table; the 31-day figure is the FY2025 all-funds Day's Net Cash ratio (adjusted).
