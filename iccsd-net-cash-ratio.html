Day's Net Cash Ratio — Iowa City vs. peers

The district's own liquidity KPI — how many days it could keep paying the bills on cash on hand — computed across the large Iowa districts, FY2015–FY2026 · June 2026

What it is: Iowa City tracks a "Day's Net Cash Ratio" on its own financial dashboard. It answers the most concrete liquidity question — how many days the district could carry expenditures without any new cash coming in.

Day's Net Cash Ratio = Cash & InvestmentsTotal Expenditures ÷ 365

The recommended range is 90–120 days (shaded green). That is the district's own target (90 days) and reflects Iowa's seasonal property-tax timing — districts receive tax money in big lumps in fall and spring, so they need a larger buffer than GFOA's general ~60-day floor (gray line) to bridge the gap.

Iowa City sat inside or near the 90–120 recommended band a decade ago (peaking at ~88 days in 2017), then fell steadily out of it. Its most recent actual year, FY2025, sits at ~33 days — about a third of the recommended minimum, and below even the 60-day GFOA floor — while large-district peers have generally stayed at or above the band (peer average ~87 days). The dashed point is the district's own FY2026 projection (~37 days); read it with care.*

*The FY2026 bump is borrowed. The district's projected ~37 days exists only because of a planned $25M revenue-anticipation warrant plus a $10M interfund loan. PFM's projected June 30, 2026 balance before the warrant is $4.4M ≈ 7 days — which PFM states is "not sufficient to cover July 2026 payroll." So the line ticking up is the borrowing, not a recovery.
