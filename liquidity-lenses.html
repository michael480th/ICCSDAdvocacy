Two Liquidity Lenses, Side by Side

Reserves vs. cash — how Moody's actually scores a school district's liquidity, and why you have to read both · June 2026

Moody's — the agency that rated ICCSD — measures liquidity with two ratios, on purpose, because each hides what the other reveals:

Reserves — Available Fund Balance ÷ revenue

The accounting cushion. It counts receivables — money owed to the district (taxes, state aid) but not yet collected — so it can look healthy even when the bank account is thin.

Cash — Net Cash ÷ revenue

The money actually on hand. Moody's subtracts short-term borrowing (tax-anticipation notes), so cash propped up by a loan doesn't count. The truest immediate-liquidity test.

Both are scored on the same scale (Aaa = strongest … Caa = weakest). Below, every district's two ratios sit back-to-back: reserves grow left, cash grows right. FY2023, the latest year all 13 have an audit.

Moody's band: Aaa (≥17.5%) Aa (10–17.5%) A (5–10%) Baa (0–5%) Ba or below (<0%)

How the story changes when you see both

One lens alone understates the problem. On reserves, Iowa City is alone at the bottom — 2.4% (Baa), the only district below the A band and ranked #13 of 13. Switch to cash and it looks less dire — 9.1% (A), still last (#13) but not in a category by itself. A reader shown only the cash number might shrug.

Seeing both reveals why that comfort is false. Notice the gap between each district's two bars. Peers' cash sits far to the right of their reserves (median gap +9 points) — they hold real surplus cash on top of healthy reserves. Iowa City's gap is the smallest in the group (+6.7): it has barely more cash than its (already thin) reserves. And this cash figure is before Moody's subtracts tax-anticipation borrowing. Because Iowa City is the district leaning on that borrowing, the subtraction hits it hardest — pulling its cash lens back down toward, or below, its weak reserve lens. Either way it finishes last; together, the two lenses show the cash "cushion" is thin and partly borrowed.

⚠ The Net Cash figures are pre-TAN. We don't yet have each district's short-term operating debt outstanding at June 30, so the right-hand bars use cash only. Subtracting tax-anticipation / cash-flow notes — as Moody's does — would shorten the cash bars, most for districts that rely on that borrowing. For Iowa City, an outstanding TAN on the order of its operating gap would erase the cash bar entirely. We'll wire in the exact figures once we have the year-end note balances.
