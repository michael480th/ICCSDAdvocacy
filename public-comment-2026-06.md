# Public Comment — ICCSD Board of Directors

Good evening. My name is Michael Parrott. I live in Coralville, and I went to Kirkwood, Northwest, and West High.

A while back, I asked this board and this district to publish the financial data in one place, where anyone could analyze it. That didn't happen — so I built it myself. It's online now, it's free to use, and it cites its sources. I'd encourage anyone — the media, the community, the state, or the district itself — to use it to look closely at how this district is performing. It will keep growing.

Gathering the facts matters. Here's why.

This district educates about 14,400 students and runs on roughly $200 million a year, with more than $300 million in debt. And right now, no one can tell you exactly how much is in the bank. The date we'll finally have audited financials has changed at nearly every board meeting. The best estimate now is 2027 at the earliest.

When I was a kid, my parents balanced their checkbook every month. In my own company, I reconcile the books every month — it's how I know what I owe in taxes. At some point, this district stopped doing the equivalent of that. That is why, at one point, it could not tell you how much it owed on its own bond payments.

I want to be clear about one thing: this is not a hard year that hit every district the same way. It is an outlier. I'll give you three facts. All three are in the repository, with their sources.

First: for fiscal year 2025, 266 Iowa school districts filed their audited financials. Six did not. Iowa City is one of the six — and the only large one. The other five are small districts, most with a few hundred students. Every other large district in Iowa — through these same years, the same state funding, the same pandemic aftermath — closed its books and filed on time. We are the outlier.

Second: a bond rating is a measure of trust. Of the thousands of school districts that carry one, only a small number lose it in any given year — almost always over missing financial information — and the overwhelming majority earn it back within months. Iowa City lost its rating in 2024 and is now heading into a second and third year without one. For a district this size, that is exceptionally rare.

Third: when a rating is withdrawn, federal securities rules — the SEC's continuing-disclosure requirements — give a district ten business days to notify the public. Iowa City's notice came roughly five hundred days later.

And I didn't just gather Iowa City's numbers — I lined them up against every other large district in Iowa, on the same basic measures, and put the charts online. Iowa City came in last, and not by a little. Two examples: it has about nine days of reserves on hand, when sixty is considered healthy; and in 2023 its spending authority went negative — the only district in the group — the level that, by law, forces a state-supervised recovery. The charts and the sources are all in the report.

None of these are matters of opinion. They are the basics: a monthly close, reconciled accounts, audited financials filed on time, a credit rating kept in good standing. Districts running far less money than we do manage every one of these, every year.

I'm not here only to point at problems. I've sent each of you a list of 25 specific actions, and I've made it public. None of it is radical. It is the basic blocking and tackling that any organization running this kind of money would already be doing.

I'll say one thing about the leadership transition. The cleanest path forward is one where the outgoing administration exits cleanly, so that whoever comes next has the room and the authority to actually fix this.

And if anyone inside the district, or anyone in this community, wants to talk in confidence — you can reach me directly. I'll keep it private.

One last thing. I don't believe anyone on this board or in district leadership did this on purpose. I believe you are good people who took these seats to serve. The oath that came with those seats asks one thing of you now: that you look honestly at what went wrong, even when the honest answer is a hard one. That is all I'm asking you to show tonight.
