# Research notes — ICCSD board financial fluency & oversight capacity

**Status:** working research file (not for publication yet). Compiled June 2026.
**Purpose:** test the hypothesis that the board's financial-oversight capacity *declined* over
time (i.e., "it used to have legit finance people / the finance oversight committee stopped
meeting"), and document what the record actually supports — so the narrative can make, at most,
a careful, defensible remark about fiduciary capacity.

> ⚠️ Sourcing caveat: board-member biographical details below come from election coverage and
> the district site, several retrieved via search-engine summaries (the Gazette and Ballotpedia
> block direct fetching). Treat individual bios as **medium confidence** until confirmed against a
> primary bio. The structural conclusions are higher confidence because they hold across many
> independent sources.

---

## TL;DR — the hypothesis was half right, and the accurate version is stronger

The initial framing had two parts; the evidence **corrects both** and points to a cleaner story:

1. **"The board used to have legit finance people."** → *Not supported.* Across every board roster
   I can document (2013 → 2026), **no member has a professional accounting / CPA / banking /
   corporate-finance background.** Backgrounds cluster in education, healthcare, law, and university
   administration. The board's lack of independent financial expertise looks **long-standing, not a
   recent decline.**
2. **"The finance oversight committee stopped meeting."** → *Not supported as stated.* There is **no
   evidence of a standing district finance/audit oversight committee before 2026.** A community
   **financial oversight committee was *created* in 2026 — *after* and *because of* the crisis.** So it
   didn't lapse; it didn't exist until the failure forced it into being.

**What *is* strongly supported** is a decline in the **professional staff** finance function, plus the
**absence of any independent check** at the board/committee layer:

- The district once produced a rigorous **Annual Financial Health Report** (FY2019, 22 pp.,
  prepared by CFO **Leslie Finger**) using the full 10-ratio framework with rating-agency framing —
  proof the *staff* monitoring capacity existed. Finger retired **June 30, 2023.**
- It was succeeded by a CFO (**Adam Kurth**) the state later noted **lacked the school-business-official
  credential**; reconciliations and monthly reporting then broke down for ~3 years; Kurth left Nov 2025.
- So at the moment operations broke down, **no layer of the system had both the capacity and the mandate
  to catch it**: a board without independent financial expertise, **no** finance oversight committee, and a
  professional finance office that had lost its experienced leadership.

That three-layer gap — not a single villain — is the defensible "fiduciary / financial-knowledge"
finding. The narrative can gesture at it carefully; this file holds the support.

**Observation:** education (Abraham, Lingo, Horn-Frasier), healthcare (Finch, Eastham), law (Williams),

administrative/financial role — not professional accounting/finance. **No CPA, accountant, banker, or
corporate-finance professional on the current board.**

*Fairness note:* school-board members are not required to be financial professionals — that is precisely
why districts rely on a qualified CFO/business official and (ideally) an independent finance committee.
The point is not that these individuals are unqualified to serve; it is that **the board had no internal
financial-expertise check** to compensate when the staff function failed.

## 3. Finance oversight committee — created *after* the crisis (2026)

- A **community financial oversight committee** (members with finance, business, and law expertise) was
  **established in 2026** to advise the board — described in coverage as "the latest step in addressing a
  crisis that exposed years of inaccurate spending and revenue tracking."
- Stated purpose: assist the board with its **fiduciary responsibilities under Iowa law**; provide
  independent, informed oversight of the district's financial condition; review the proposed budget before
  board approval; monitor performance vs. budget; review multi-year projections. Authority to request
  information from staff and **meet with auditors / management / legal counsel.** Meets **≥6×/year.**
- Board director **Mitch Lingo** discussed bringing in outside finance/accounting/business experts and
  increasing meeting frequency.
- **No evidence found of a prior standing finance/audit committee** (2013–2025). Coverage uniformly frames
  the 2026 body as *new*. → The "stopped meeting" premise should be **dropped or re-sourced** before use.

## 4. The staff finance function — where the decline is real (cross-ref to news file)

(Documented in the 2026 news index and the FY24 audit; summarized here because it carries the actual
decline.)

- **Leslie Finger**, CFO, authored the **FY2019 Annual Financial Health Report** (rigorous 10-ratio
  monitoring) → retired **June 30, 2023**; later emails (Gazette) show he had flagged ~$500K tax penalties,
  missing reports, and a growing special-ed deficit on the way out.
- **Adam Kurth** succeeded him — the state's bureau chief later testified the person serving as school
  business official **"was not actually their financial leader"** and lacked the predecessor's skill set
  (Iowa Code 272.31 credential issue). Kurth resigned **Nov 2025.**
- **Alan Moran** (Director of Financial Reporting, joined Sept 2024) warned leadership of **"fundamental
  errors"** in **Oct 2024**; no documented response from the superintendent/deputy.
- **Pat Moore** (former Solon CFO, 28 yrs school finance) hired **April 2026** to rebuild the function.
- Result: bank reconciliations not done ~3 years; monthly board financial reporting incomplete/unreliable;
  FY23 audit filed 26 months late; the **$10M interfund loan (Aug 2025) omitted from a quarterly report.**

## 5. Synthesis — the three-layer oversight gap

At the moment the finances broke down, **none of the three layers that should catch this was functioning:**

1. **Professional staff** — lost its experienced CFO (Finger, 2023); successor under-credentialed; controls
   (reconciliation, monthly reporting, segregation of duties) collapsed.
2. **Independent committee** — none existed until one was created *after* the crisis (2026).
3. **The board itself** — structurally lacked independent financial expertise to question the numbers, and
   was, by the SBRC's account, operating on **"bad information… not complete, not accurate, not viable."**

That is the careful, sourced version of "a severe lack of fiduciary capacity and financial knowledge":
not that anyone was necessarily corrupt, but that **the system had no functioning financial check at any
level** — which is exactly how a 9% payroll overrun and a $10M unauthorized loan go unnoticed.
