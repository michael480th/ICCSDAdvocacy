# SAVE spending, SAVE budget, How much money is left? Capital Projects Wants v. Needs,

**From:** [REDACTED SENDER]  
**To:** Board  
**Date:** October 21, 2024 at 2:20 PM

Dear Board Members,

My children have graduated; however, since I could have grandchildren go to school here someday and viewed your *Center for Innovation Pathways to Purpose PowerPoint* presentation, I am writing to ask you to **<u>NOT support or pay for a remodel at the Center for Innovation for the following reasons.</u>**

1. First and foremost, I would not have wanted my own students to spend more time on screens. It hampers concentration. It is ironic to put students on more screens at a time when you're considering limiting cell phone use. If career information is valuable for students to have, come up with other less expensive, less screen-oriented ways to teach and share it and consider using the schools you have to do so.

2. The financial audit for fiscal year 2023 is not back yet. Fiscal year 2022's audit was filed by February 15, 2023.

3. ICCSD has competing capital needs and no public long-term financial budget for SAVE. **<u>The public has not been told how much SAVE money has been spent and committed, nor has the public been informed how much SAVE money is is left and available to spend through 2050.</u>** Competing priorities--preschool center(s), Coralville athletic facility, other athletic facilities, future building and repair needs, etc.--should be prioritized and budgeted for now **<u>before</u>** SAVE dollars/capital funds are committed.

   a. What SAVE dollars will be kept in reserve for unanticipated future needs?

   b. Is the current SAVE reserve, if any, and on top of what the state requires, sufficient?

   Please provide a **<u>SAVE budget and SAVE financial projections to 2050</u>** and have serious **<u>public discussions</u>** involving the community about **<u>competing future priorities and needs (not wants)</u>** that will require SAVE dollars.

4. If ICCSD has capital needs, including big ticket maintenance items, in the future before 2050 and runs out of SAVE dollars, ICCSD has two primary potential sources of money

   a. ICCSD can have bond vote, which may be difficult to pass with vouchers, or

   b. ICCSD can sell school buildings because the proceeds are deposited in the PPEL fund.

**<u>Schools should not close in the future because ICCSD is spending SAVE/capital project dollars now on current wants instead of needs and then has so little capital project money for the future, it is forced to sell school buildings to pay for them.</u>**

5. I do **<u>not</u>** believe the statement in the PowerPoint that these wants are "All capital costs, no general fund impact." At a minimum, ICCSD will spend general fund dollars on bussing, housekeeping, and employee time. These general fund dollars would be better directed toward providing, for example, more students with Spanish, which is a real-world employment skill. Just because Junior Achievement can raise money for five years for ICCSD (assuming it can be done) does **<u>not</u>** mean ICCSD should move forward with this project. An evaluation should also be done to see if Junior Achievement raising money for school wants impacts ICCSD and the foundation's ability to raise money for actual school needs and not wants.

6. If this project is so vital, which it is NOT, then it is one where the capital expense should be shared by other school districts.

7. With vouchers, ICCSD needs to compete with private schools. I would have moved my children for better academics but **<u>not</u>** this renovation.

8. Voting yes on this capital spending and other big-ticket items hamstrings future boards' ability to spend SAVE dollars on future needs out to the year 2050. Not having money for future capital projects could hurt ICCSD's ability to be competitive long term.

I realize that the PowerPoint is a presentation; however, please examine and publicly discuss all potential future competing SAVE funded projects and put together a budget for SAVE to 2050. Also consider not spending money for at least a couple of years to determine what exactly is a capital project need that will improve academic outcomes and not a want and how much money is left.

Thank you for your service.

Sincerely,

[REDACTED SENDER NAME]
