# ICCSD 2023 News Coverage

A curated index of news coverage of the Iowa City Community School District and its Board of Directors published during calendar year 2023. Each entry includes the publication, byline (where available), publication date, a summary of substantive content, selected short quotes attributed to the original reporters and sources, and a direct link to the original article. Articles are arranged in chronological order where date is available, with year-spanning coverage grouped at the end.

> **A note on reproduction:** This document contains summaries and brief quoted excerpts only. Full article text is not reproduced; readers should follow the links to read the original reporting at each outlet. Copyright remains with the respective publishers (KCRG, The Cedar Rapids Gazette, The Daily Iowan, CBS2 Iowa / KGAN, Iowa Capital Dispatch, Little Village, Press-Citizen, Corridor Business, and others).

> **2023 framing.** 2023 was a year of *quiet structural shifts* that would shape every subsequent year of ICCSD coverage:
>
> 1. **The ESA era begins.** On January 24, 2023 — early in his first month — Gov. Kim Reynolds signed the Students First Act creating Iowa's universal Education Savings Account program. The 2023–24 school year saw 16,757 students use ESAs to attend accredited Iowa private schools; ICCSD's resulting revenue loss compounded across the following years.
> 2. **A two-year budget-cutting cycle starts.** The board approved a roughly $319 million FY24 budget plan that included staffing reductions of 18 elementary teachers and 9 secondary teachers (~$2.5M in savings spread over two years) and reduced employee benefit contributions. Hills Elementary closure was first publicly considered as part of this cycle; the formal closure vote came in March 2024.
> 3. **A quiet CFO transition with later consequences.** Long-serving CFO Leslie Finger retired June 30, 2023. Adam Kurth — previously the district's director of technology — moved into the CFO role without a formal accounting credential. (Per retrospective Gazette reporting in March 2026, Finger emailed Kurth across 2023–24 voicing concerns about late federal tax filings, the absence of routine financial reports, and growth in the special-education deficit.) Kurth would remain CFO until November 2025, when he resigned shortly before the financial crisis became public.
> 4. **A board reset.** The Nov 7, 2023 election seated the "W.A.L.E." slate — Williams, Abraham, Lingo, Eastham — and Maka Pilcher Hayek did not seek re-election. The board that took office in December 2023 was the board that would carry ICCSD into the Hills closure, the Center for Innovation rollout, the ESA fallout, the AEA-law transition, and (in 2026) the financial crisis itself.

## January 2023 — A new state policy environment

### "Iowa Gov. Reynolds signs the Students First Act into law"
**Governor Kim Reynolds' office / statewide press coverage** — January 24, 2023

A foundational item for everything that follows in ICCSD's funding trajectory. Reynolds signs Iowa's universal Education Savings Account legislation, making state-funded scholarships available for eligible Iowa K-12 students to attend accredited private schools. Reference materials and program description: [Iowa Department of Education ESA page](https://educate.iowa.gov/pk-12/educational-choice/education-savings-accounts).

The first year of program operation (the 2023–24 school year) saw 16,757 students using ESAs. ICCSD's estimated direct revenue loss from ESA take-up was roughly $1.3 million — a figure that would be the headline of Daily Iowan and Gazette reporting in April 2024.

### Regular board meetings — January 23 and January 31, 2023
**ICCSD Board of Directors** (board video/minutes — see local archive)

Two early-year regular meetings: a strategic-planning conversation kicking off the calendar and a follow-up. (Original district board recordings and agendas are catalogued in the local ICCSD archive folder for 2023.)

## April — May 2023 — Spring 2023 budget cuts

### "Iowa City schools reduce staff as pandemic funding expires"
**The Cedar Rapids Gazette**
[https://www.thegazette.com/k/iowa-city-schools-reduce-staff-as-pandemic-funding-expires/](https://www.thegazette.com/k/iowa-city-schools-reduce-staff-as-pandemic-funding-expires/)

The defining spring 2023 budget piece. The district plans to reduce 18 elementary teachers and 9 secondary teachers — about $2.5 million in savings phased over two years — alongside reduced employee benefit contributions and the early-retirement program (about $1.5 million in savings from nine positions). FY24 budget framework: a roughly $319 million plan, including the new facility master plan. Coverage points to the end of federal pandemic funding and per-pupil state aid that isn't keeping pace with inflation as the dominant pressure.

### Regular board meetings — April 25, May 9, May 23, 2023
**ICCSD Board of Directors**

Three end-of-year meetings carrying the FY24 budget through adoption, contracts, and end-of-year staff actions. The May 23 meeting (249 KB JSON in the local archive) is the more substantial of the three.

## June — July 2023 — CFO transition (largely undocumented in news at the time)

### CFO transition — Finger retires; Kurth steps in (June 30, 2023)
*Not the subject of a contemporaneous 2023 news article in this collection.*
Most authoritative retrospective: [The Cedar Rapids Gazette — Former Iowa City school CFO questioned district's finances](https://www.thegazette.com/news/education/emails-former-iowa-city-cfo-questioned-school-finances-months-before-crisis/article_62726404-394c-4e0f-aae0-1b142bd58f07.html)

Long-serving CFO **Leslie Finger** retires on June 30, 2023. Director of Technology **Adam Kurth** moves into the CFO role. Per the Gazette's 2026 investigative reporting, Kurth had no degree in accounting or other financial qualifications. The same emails reviewed by the Gazette show Finger writing to Kurth across the following year voicing concerns about roughly $500,000 in federal tax penalty payments, missing financial reports, and growth in the special-education deficit "by millions of dollars." None of this was publicly known at the time.

### Regular board meetings — June 13, June 27, 2023
**ICCSD Board of Directors**

Two end-of-FY meetings (the June 27 session is 280 KB JSON), which would include FY23 close-out items and FY24 organizational decisions — including the CFO transition.

The newly elected board took office at the December 2023 reorganization meeting. **Shawn Eyestone** continued as Board President; **Lisa Williams** continued as Vice President. The configuration carried forward through 2024 until Claussen's spring 2024 resignation prompted the Eyestone-appointment / Abraham-as-VP shift documented in `../2024/News_2024.md`.

## Year-spanning items also referenced in 2023 coverage

### Center for Innovation programming (development phase)
**Corridor Business Journal**
[https://corridorbusiness.com/iccsds-center-for-innovation-taking-shape/](https://corridorbusiness.com/iccsds-center-for-innovation-taking-shape/)

ICCSD purchased the former ACT Tyler Building for $8.7 million in 2022; 2023 was the planning year for what would become the Center for Innovation. The district engaged educators and the local business community in conversations about programming during 2023; the public rollout of Junior Achievement / Kirkwood Career Academy programming and the "ICCSD Innovates" branding came in 2024–25.

### Strategic-planning consulting
*Referenced in retrospective Center for Innovation coverage.*
The district engaged Trace Pickering — cofounder of Cedar Rapids' Iowa BIG project-based learning center — as a Center for Innovation consultant during 2023.

### ESA program reference materials (state-level)
[Iowa Department of Education — ESA page](https://educate.iowa.gov/pk-12/educational-choice/education-savings-accounts)
[EdChoice fiscal brief on Iowa ESA (April 2024 retrospective)](https://www.edchoice.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/IA-Fiscal-Brief.pdf)
[Iowa Capital Dispatch — Iowa ESA program linked to private school tuition spike (May 23, 2024)](https://iowacapitaldispatch.com/2024/05/23/study-links-state-esa-program-to-spike-in-iowa-k-12-private-school-tuition/)

For readers interested in the policy mechanics: an academic study links Iowa's ESA program to a 21–25% increase in kindergarten private-school tuition prices (universal-eligibility grade level) and 10–16% in partial-eligibility grades. The first year of the program generated approximately $16.7 million in net fiscal cost to state and local taxpayers combined.
