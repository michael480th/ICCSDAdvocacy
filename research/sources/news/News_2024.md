# ICCSD 2024 News Coverage

A curated index of news coverage of the Iowa City Community School District and its Board of Directors published during calendar year 2024. Each entry includes the publication, byline (where available), publication date, a summary of substantive content, selected short quotes attributed to the original reporters and sources, and a direct link to the original article. Articles are arranged in chronological order.

> **A note on reproduction:** This document contains summaries and brief quoted excerpts only. Full article text is not reproduced; readers should follow the links to read the original reporting at each outlet. Copyright remains with the respective publishers (KCRG, The Cedar Rapids Gazette, The Daily Iowan, CBS2 Iowa / KGAN, Iowa Public Radio, Iowa Capital Dispatch, Little Village, West Side Story, and others).

> **2024 framing.** Three story arcs shape 2024 coverage of ICCSD:
>
> 1. **The Hills Elementary closure.** A March 2024 unanimous board vote closed Hills Elementary at the end of the 2023–24 school year — saving roughly $1.66 million and serving as the symbolic centerpiece of two years of cost-cutting. The closure unfolded across February (community opposition), March (vote), April (transition to Alexander Elementary), and August (demolition vote).
> 2. **Statewide policy turbulence flowing through ICCSD.** Gov. Reynolds signed the AEA-reform law on March 27, 2024, restructuring how Area Education Agencies are funded — a change that takes full effect in FY26 and shapes ICCSD's funding flexibility. Iowa's private-school Education Savings Account program drained roughly $1.3 million from ICCSD's revenue in 2024. A new state law prompted the district's May 2024 decision to opt out of having School Resource Officers in buildings. A 2025 state cell-phone-ban law was foreshadowed by district-level cell-phone-policy work all autumn 2024.
> 3. **A board-membership transition that quietly mattered.** Board VP J.P. Claussen resigned in spring 2024 to take a teaching job at West High; the board appointed former director Shawn Eyestone in June 2024 to fill the seat (he would later be the third seat on the November 2025 ballot, then retire in December 2025). Molly Abraham was elevated to vice president. The roster shifts of 2024 set the board configuration that would face the financial crisis disclosed in January 2026.

## February 2024

### "Iowa City School District lays out plans to 'retire' Hills Elementary"
**KCRG-TV9** — February 28, 2024
[https://www.kcrg.com/2024/02/28/iowa-city-school-district-lays-out-plans-retire-hills-elementary/](https://www.kcrg.com/2024/02/28/iowa-city-school-district-lays-out-plans-retire-hills-elementary/)

District administration formally introduces a proposal to "retire" Hills Elementary — the district's smallest school by enrollment (about 150 students) — at the end of the 2023–24 school year. The word *retire* is chosen deliberately to signal a phased, considered closure. Estimated savings: roughly $1.66 million of the $3.75 million the district needs to trim from FY25.

### "Parents, students against potential closure of Iowa City schools' Hills Elementary"
**The Daily Iowan** — February 27, 2024
[https://dailyiowan.com/2024/02/27/parents-students-against-potential-closure-of-iowa-city-schools-hills-elementary/](https://dailyiowan.com/2024/02/27/parents-students-against-potential-closure-of-iowa-city-schools-hills-elementary/)

The community-response piece. Parents and students from Hills attend the public-comment portion of board meetings urging directors to reject the closure proposal. Coverage emphasizes the small school's cohesive community feel and the distance Hills families would have to travel to consolidate at Alexander Elementary.

## March 2024

### "Iowa City School Board President discusses retirement of Hills Elementary"
**KCRG-TV9** — March 1, 2024
[https://www.kcrg.com/2024/03/01/iowa-city-school-board-president-discusses-retirement-hills-elementary/](https://www.kcrg.com/2024/03/01/iowa-city-school-board-president-discusses-retirement-hills-elementary/)

KCRG interview with then–board president Ruthina Malone on the Hills decision. The conversation establishes the case the board would lean on: the closure is the least bad option among bad options driven by the end of federal pandemic funding, declining enrollment, and per-pupil state aid that isn't keeping pace with cost growth.

**The Daily Iowan** — March 25, 2024

Pre-vote curtain-raiser. Lays out the cost case for closure ($1.66M of $3.75M needed for FY25) and previews community testimony expected at the March 26 meeting.

### "ICCSD Board to vote on closing Hills Elementary Tuesday"
**KCRG-TV9** — March 26, 2024
[https://www.kcrg.com/2024/03/26/iccsd-board-vote-closing-hills-elementary-tuesday/](https://www.kcrg.com/2024/03/26/iccsd-board-vote-closing-hills-elementary-tuesday/)

KCRG's day-of preview.

### "Iowa City school board votes to close Hills Elementary to save $1.6 million"
**The Daily Iowan** — March 26, 2024
[https://dailyiowan.com/2024/03/26/iowa-city-school-board-votes-to-close-hills-elementary-to-save-1-6-million/](https://dailyiowan.com/2024/03/26/iowa-city-school-board-votes-to-close-hills-elementary-to-save-1-6-million/)

The decision: the board votes unanimously to close Hills Elementary at the end of the 2023–24 school year. The story attributes the broader spending pressure to "chronic and persistent underfunding" of Iowa public schools (a quote attributed to board member Lisa Williams in this and related Gazette coverage). Over the prior three years, $24.7 million has been reduced from the district's spending plan.

### "Hills Elementary to close at the end of the school year, Iowa City school board decides"
**The Cedar Rapids Gazette**
[https://www.thegazette.com/k/hills-elementary-to-close-at-the-end-of-the-school-year-iowa-city-school-board-decides/](https://www.thegazette.com/k/hills-elementary-to-close-at-the-end-of-the-school-year-iowa-city-school-board-decides/)

The Gazette's companion piece. Confirms the unanimous vote and outlines the cost-reduction package: closing Hills, delaying curriculum purchases, adjusting the class-size model, and reducing staff through attrition — together totaling a $5.5 million general-fund reduction.

### "Gov. Reynolds signs AEA bill 'that no one really likes'"
**Iowa Starting Line** — March 26, 2024
[https://iowastartingline.com/2024/03/26/aea-bill-that-no-one-really-likes-passes-senate-heads-to-reynolds-for-signing/](https://iowastartingline.com/2024/03/26/aea-bill-that-no-one-really-likes-passes-senate-heads-to-reynolds-for-signing/)

Statewide-policy story relevant to ICCSD. The Iowa Senate passes the AEA-reform measure (HF 2612) 30–18; the bill heads to Gov. Reynolds. The framing of the headline foreshadows two years of unsettled implementation.

### "Iowa Gov. Kim Reynolds signs AEA changes, teacher salary increases into law"
**Iowa Capital Dispatch** — March 27, 2024
[https://iowacapitaldispatch.com/2024/03/27/iowa-gov-kim-reynolds-signs-aea-changes-teacher-salary-increases-into-law/](https://iowacapitaldispatch.com/2024/03/27/iowa-gov-kim-reynolds-signs-aea-changes-teacher-salary-increases-into-law/)

Gov. Reynolds signs HF 2612 the day after Senate passage. The law shifts funding for Iowa's Area Education Agencies to local school districts and moves oversight of special education to a state agency; it also raises Iowa teacher starting salaries to $47,500 (effective July 1, 2024) and to $50,000 a year later.

### "After months of talks and debate, Reynolds signs AEA overhaul"
**Iowa Public Radio** — March 28, 2024
[https://www.iowapublicradio.org/state-government-news/2024-03-28/after-months-of-talks-and-debate-reynolds-signs-aea-overhaul](https://www.iowapublicradio.org/state-government-news/2024-03-28/after-months-of-talks-and-debate-reynolds-signs-aea-overhaul)

IPR's recap of the AEA law's path to signature, with stakeholder reaction.

### "$5.5 million cut from Iowa City schools budget next year"
**The Cedar Rapids Gazette**
[https://www.thegazette.com/k/5-5-million-cut-from-iowa-city-schools-budget-next-year/](https://www.thegazette.com/k/5-5-million-cut-from-iowa-city-schools-budget-next-year/)

The Gazette's spring 2024 budget-adoption piece. The FY25 budget is approximately $195 million (a 6.5% decrease versus the prior fiscal year). The cuts include closing Hills, delaying curriculum purchases, adjusting class-size methodology, and attrition-based staff reductions — together $5.5 million. Underlying drivers: end of federal pandemic funding, a 2.5% per-pupil state aid increase that doesn't keep pace with employee health insurance cost growth of 8–15%.

## December 2024 — Cell-phone policy adoption

### "Iowa City Community School District unveils potential cell phone policy"
**KCRG-TV9** — December 10, 2024
[https://www.kcrg.com/2024/12/10/iowa-city-schools-unveil-potential-cell-phone-policy/](https://www.kcrg.com/2024/12/10/iowa-city-schools-unveil-potential-cell-phone-policy/)

The district unveils the policy text the board will consider for adoption: phones and headphones not allowed in class, not to be used, heard, or seen from the late bell to end of class; devices kept in a bag or locker; use permitted only at lunch and between classes. First-offense / repeat-offense enforcement is the same: confiscation to the office for the remainder of the school day.

### "Iowa City school district implements personal device policy for schools"
**The Daily Iowan** — December 10, 2024
[https://dailyiowan.com/2024/12/10/iowa-city-school-district-implements-personal-device-policy-for-schools/](https://dailyiowan.com/2024/12/10/iowa-city-school-district-implements-personal-device-policy-for-schools/)

The Daily Iowan's policy explainer covering middle- and high-school provisions: limited access during non-instructional moments — passing time, lunch, and (for high school) open hours in designated areas and study hall.
