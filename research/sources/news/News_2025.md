# ICCSD 2025 News Coverage

A curated index of news coverage of the Iowa City Community School District and its Board of Directors published during calendar year 2025. Each entry includes the publication, byline (where available), publication date, a summary of substantive content, selected short quotes attributed to the original reporters and sources, and a direct link to the original article. Articles are arranged in chronological order.

> **A note on reproduction:** This document contains summaries and brief quoted excerpts only. Full article text is not reproduced; readers should follow the links to read the original reporting at each outlet. Copyright remains with the respective publishers (KCRG, The Cedar Rapids Gazette, The Daily Iowan, CBS2 Iowa / KGAN, Iowa Public Radio, Little Village, West Side Story, The Little Hawk, and others).

> **2025 framing.** 2025 was the year *before* ICCSD's financial crisis became public. With the benefit of hindsight (see `../2026/News_2026.md`), three things define the year:
>
> 1. **An "official" narrative of financial recovery.** In April 2025, then-CFO Adam Kurth told the board the district was past the worst of the multi-year cutting cycle, with the unspent balance projected to reach roughly $7 million by June 30, 2025. Coverage in this period framed ICCSD as "not in a cutting mode."
> 2. **An "actual" narrative no one saw at the time.** In August 2025, the district's financial team transferred $10 million from the health insurance fund to the general fund to cover payroll, without board notification — a fact that did not surface publicly until late January 2026. There is no 2025 news coverage of this event. It existed as a financial fact during 2025 but as a public story only in retrospect.
> 3. **An election, a board reorganization, and a CFO departure.** The Nov 4, 2025 election re-elected Malone and Finch and added Horn-Frasier. Shawn Eyestone retired Dec 9, 2025. CFO Adam Kurth left the district in November 2025. The board entered 2026 already having lost the staff member who managed (and miscommunicated) the August transfer.

## January 2025

### "Iowa City school district demolishes the former Hills Elementary building"
**The Daily Iowan** — January 21, 2025
[https://dailyiowan.com/2025/01/21/iowa-city-school-district-demolishes-the-former-hills-elementary-building/](https://dailyiowan.com/2025/01/21/iowa-city-school-district-demolishes-the-former-hills-elementary-building/)

The shuttered Hills Elementary building comes down — a physical postscript to the March 2024 board decision to close it. ICCSD closed Hills at the end of the 2023–24 school year to save roughly $1.66 million, part of a roughly $24.7 million in reductions across the prior three years (per Gazette retrospective coverage). The demolition story serves as the symbolic close of one budget-cutting era — and, in hindsight, the calm before the next.

### "ICCSD implements new phone policy"
**West Side Story** (Iowa City West High School student newspaper)
[https://wsspaper.com/100712/news/iccsd-implements-new-phone-policy/](https://wsspaper.com/100712/news/iccsd-implements-new-phone-policy/)

A new district-wide cell phone policy goes into effect for middle and high schools on January 21, 2025. Under the policy, electronics must be put away during instructional time; high schoolers may use phones during passing periods, lunch, study hall, and open hours; middle schoolers may use phones during passing periods and lunch but not during study hall.

### Companion pieces — Dialing down on phones / Screen time's over
**West Side Story**
[https://wsspaper.com/107338/feature/dialing-down-on-phones/](https://wsspaper.com/107338/feature/dialing-down-on-phones/)
[https://wsspaper.com/109638/showcase/screen-times-over-the-effects-of-the-iccsds-phone-policy/](https://wsspaper.com/109638/showcase/screen-times-over-the-effects-of-the-iccsds-phone-policy/)

Two West Side Story features tracking student and staff reaction to the new policy over the spring 2025 semester. The student paper's reporting is consistent: the policy is working better than students expected.

## Spring 2025 (March–April)

### "Iowa City schools' budget: 'Not in a cutting mode'"
**The Cedar Rapids Gazette**
[https://www.thegazette.com/k/after-two-years-of-budget-cuts-including-closing-hills-elementary-school-iowa-city-schools-not-i/](https://www.thegazette.com/k/after-two-years-of-budget-cuts-including-closing-hills-elementary-school-iowa-city-schools-not-i/)

The headline-defining 2025 budget piece. After two years of reductions — including the Hills Elementary closure — the Gazette reports the district is "not in a cutting mode" for the current fiscal year. The piece also notes the district is "not in a growth mode either" given projected enrollment declines tied to a falling area birth rate. Then-CFO Adam Kurth told the board he expected the district to see roughly a 50-student enrollment decline for the 2025–26 school year.

The "unspent balance" — a key liquidity measure for Iowa school districts — was projected to reach about $7 million by the end of FY25 (June 30, 2025), up from a recent low point in negative territory. This was the basis for the district's optimistic spring framing.

### "Iowa City CSD approves 'reasonably healthy' $40M budget decrease, touts record enrollment"
**Yahoo News (syndicated; original reporting from Iowa City Press-Citizen)**
[https://www.yahoo.com/news/iowa-city-csd-approves-reasonably-204845348.html](https://www.yahoo.com/news/iowa-city-csd-approves-reasonably-204845348.html)

Spring 2025 budget-adoption coverage. The board approves a "reasonably healthy" budget for FY26. Under the proposed budget, the property-tax levy for district homeowners would be $16.10 per $1,000 of taxable valuation, a decrease of about 72 cents from the prior year — roughly $1.50/month less for a homeowner with a $200,000 home. The story emphasizes record enrollment claims and a stabilizing financial picture. (This same April 2025 framing would, by January 2026, look very different in light of the unreported $10M interfund loan and the spending overruns that drove it.)

## Summer 2025 (June)

### Facilities Master Plan 2.3 — board approval
*Reconstructed from Gazette and KCRG follow-up coverage in 2026; the original approval is not the subject of a single named article in this collection but is described retrospectively.*
Most authoritative retrospective: [The Gazette, "Iowa City schools pause $100 million in facility plans"](https://www.thegazette.com/news/education/iowa-city-schools-pause-100m-in-facility-plans-amid-financial-crisis/article_6b4376be-7935-4354-9d09-a75484868040.html)

In June 2025, the ICCSD board approved Facilities Master Plan 2.3, committing the district to more than $100 million in projects — including new athletic fieldhouses, indoor turf at three high schools, and a $14 million contribution toward a new Coralville Recreation Center with a 20-lane competition pool. The plan would be unanimously paused in May 2026 (see `../2026/News_2026.md`) after the financial crisis came to light.

### The unseen August transfer
*Not contemporaneously reported in 2025.*
Most authoritative retrospective: [Daily Iowan, Feb 9, 2026 — ICCSD school board to address an undisclosed $10 million transfer between district funds](https://dailyiowan.com/2026/02/09/iccsd-school-board-to-address-a-10-million-transfer-between-district-funds/)

In August 2025, the district's financial team moved $10 million from the health insurance fund to the general fund to cover payroll without board notification or approval. Search of available 2025 reporting turns up no contemporaneous news coverage of this event. The transfer would not become a public story until the January 27, 2026 board meeting. It is listed here for completeness because the transaction itself happened in 2025, even though the *story* of it is a 2026 story.

## Fall 2025

### "ICCSD takes measures to combat measles if identified within the district"
**The Daily Iowan** — September 2, 2025
[https://dailyiowan.com/2025/09/02/iccsd-takes-measures-to-combat-measles-if-identified-within-the-district/](https://dailyiowan.com/2025/09/02/iccsd-takes-measures-to-combat-measles-if-identified-within-the-district/)

Back-to-school public-health preparedness piece. The district outlines its planned response if a measles case is identified, against the backdrop of the broader 2025 national measles activity. Coverage includes immunization-policy reminders and coordination with public health.

### "With Iowa's AEA law fully in effect, small and large school districts see different impacts"
**Iowa Public Radio** — September 18, 2025
[https://www.iowapublicradio.org/education/2025-09-18/with-iowas-aea-law-fully-in-effect-small-and-large-school-districts-see-different-impacts](https://www.iowapublicradio.org/education/2025-09-18/with-iowas-aea-law-fully-in-effect-small-and-large-school-districts-see-different-impacts)

Statewide impact piece on the 2024 AEA-reform law as it kicks in for FY26. Districts now control all media and educational-services money that used to flow automatically to Area Education Agencies; they may buy services from an AEA on a fee-for-service basis, from a different vendor, or use the money for other purposes. Starting FY26, districts must contract with an AEA for 90% of the funding while keeping 10% for special-education at the district level. Coverage includes a statement from ICCSD Superintendent Matt Degner about how the law has provided "funding flexibility" — including the ability to pay for additional positions and absorb inflation-driven cost increases — at a district of more than 14,000 students.

### "ICCSD approves bids for Dream Accelerator construction"
**The Daily Iowan** — September 23, 2025
[https://dailyiowan.com/2025/09/23/iccsd-approves-bids-for-dream-accelerator-construction/](https://dailyiowan.com/2025/09/23/iccsd-approves-bids-for-dream-accelerator-construction/)

The board awards more than $500,000 to two construction firms to build out the Junior Achievement Dream Accelerator program space at the Center for Innovation (the former ACT campus, where the district relocated its administrative offices in 2025). The Center for Innovation is described in later coverage as approximately a $2.1 million investment in immersive career-exploration programming. This is one of the last "expansion" decisions before the financial picture changes.

### "ICCSD school board candidates discuss ICE protocols, school safety ahead of elections"

### "Five candidates vie for three open seats in the ICCSD school board election on Nov. 4"
**The Daily Iowan** — October 28, 2025
[https://dailyiowan.com/2025/10/28/five-candidates-vie-for-three-open-seats-in-the-iccsd-school-board-election-on-nov-4/](https://dailyiowan.com/2025/10/28/five-candidates-vie-for-three-open-seats-in-the-iccsd-school-board-election-on-nov-4/)

The pre-election race-overview piece. Five candidates running for three open seats — incumbents Malone and Finch, newcomers Jennifer Horn-Frasier, Dan Stevenson, and David Noerper. Topline candidate themes:

- *Horn-Frasier:* strategy-consulting background; founded Bluebird Sky, helped start the ACT Foundation; priorities are civil rights and safety, precise budgeting, and closing achievement gaps.
- *Stevenson:* veteran teacher (more than 20 years), West Liberty High School U.S. History teacher, former president of the West Liberty Education Association, served on the Iowa City Public Library Board of Trustees; "equitable educational opportunities" as core priority.
- *Noerper:* 18-year Iowa City resident with two children in ICCSD; priorities are safe and supportive environment for students and staff, improved communications between district leadership and school staff, and assessing classroom screen-use impact.

## December 2025 — Board reorganization and the cell-phone-ban opening

### "ICCSD school board retires Shawn Eyestone, swears in members"
**The Daily Iowan** — December 9, 2025
[https://dailyiowan.com/2025/12/09/iccsd-school-board-retires-shawn-eyestone-swears-in-members/](https://dailyiowan.com/2025/12/09/iccsd-school-board-retires-shawn-eyestone-swears-in-members/)

At the final board meeting of 2025, the board formally retires Shawn Eyestone — first elected in 2017 — and swears in the November 4 winners. Jennifer Horn-Frasier joins the board for the first time. Officer reorganization establishes the board configuration that will face the 2026 financial crisis: Ruthina Malone (president), Molly Abraham (vice president), with Eastham, Finch, Horn-Frasier, Lingo, and Williams as directors.

### "Iowa City School Board discusses possible shift to full cell phone ban policy"
**KCRG-TV9** — December 19, 2025
[https://www.kcrg.com/2025/12/19/iowa-city-school-board-discusses-possible-shift-full-cell-phone-ban-policy/](https://www.kcrg.com/2025/12/19/iowa-city-school-board-discusses-possible-shift-full-cell-phone-ban-policy/)

The board opens what will become a multi-month 2026 deliberation about moving from the January 2025 instructional-time policy to a full bell-to-bell ban. The piece reports the board's intent to consult district staff and run a study group before voting on any new changes. The deliberation continues in 2026 (see `../2026/News_2026.md`).

## Year-spanning context items also referenced in 2025 coverage

### Quiet CFO departure
*Not the subject of a stand-alone 2025 news article in this collection.*
**Adam Kurth left ICCSD in November 2025**, per retrospective Gazette reporting (Mar 2026). Kurth had been hired as CFO in 2023 from a prior role as the district's director of technology — without an accounting degree or other formal financial qualifications. His departure created an acting/interim gap from late 2025 into 2026, which was filled in February 2026 by Kim Michael-Lee (interim) and resolved in April 2026 with the hire of Pat Moore (effective July 1, 2026).
