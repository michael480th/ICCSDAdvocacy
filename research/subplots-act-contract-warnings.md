# Research notes — three sub-plots (ACT building, superintendent contract, community warnings)

**Status:** working research file. Compiled June 2026. Companion to `board-financial-oversight.md`.
**Purpose:** capture the three additional threads (capital decision / governance / "they were warned"),
mark what is confirmed vs. what needs sourcing, and set the framing so the public piece stays factual and
fair.

## Sub-plot 1 — The ACT "Tyler Building" purchase (2022)

**Confirmed (multiple outlets).**
- ICCSD bought ACT's **Tyler Building for $8.7M** (some coverage: $8.75M) in **2022**, ~85,000 SF above
  grade on **7.9 acres**, at 301 ACT Drive. Funded from **PPEL** (Physical Plant & Equipment Levy).
- It became the **Center for Innovation** and the district's central administrative offices (board meetings,
  Kirkwood classes, ICCSD Online staff).
- Coverage headline: "gets OK to spend $8.75 million on ACT building, but not without questions" — the
  purchase was publicly contested.

**Claimed in the June 14, 2022 community email (NOT yet independently confirmed).**
- The building's **assessed value was $5,365,000**, and **ACT's own 2018/2019 appraisal valued it at
  $4,555,000** — i.e., the district paid ~$8.7M for a building the owner had appraised at ~$4.6M a few years
  earlier. (Verify the assessed value via Johnson County Assessor; the appraisal figure is the writer's.)
- The **University of Iowa reportedly rejected the building as a donation.**
- **"The CEO of its owner is a former ICCSD board member."** This is the conflict-of-interest claim. It is
  the most sensitive line in the whole project. **Do not state it until a name and the board-tenure dates
  are sourced.** See research job 5.

**How to frame in the piece.** The confirmed facts (an $8.7M PPEL purchase of an admin building, above
assessed value, publicly questioned at the time) stand on their own as a capital-allocation decision worth
scrutiny. The conflict-of-interest concern should be attributed to the community email as a question raised
in real time, not asserted as fact, unless job 5 confirms it.

**Timeline irony for the narrative.** Bought the ACT building 2022 ($8.7M, PPEL). Moved admin in. Then in
**April 2026** sold the *old* HQ (1725 N Dodge) to the City of Iowa City for **$3.2M** to raise cash during
the crisis. The district spent $8.7M on a bigger admin home going into the squeeze, then sold the old one
for a fraction coming out of it.

## Sub-plot 2 — The superintendent contract

**Confirmed.**
- Degner's **3-year contract** (through June 30, 2028) was approved **unanimously** in **July 2025**, first-
  year salary **$260,494** — weeks before the crisis surfaced.
- It allows termination "by mutual agreement, by board discretion, or by superintendent discretion in May of
  each year as required by Iowa law" (the standard Iowa non-renewal window).
- When the crisis broke, the board did **not** remove him. It approved his transition to **Executive Director
  of Secondary Schools at ~$180K** (4-2 vote, 2026).

**Claimed in the June 14, 2022 community email (the substantive critique).**
- The contract has **no performance measures or accountability metrics**; is **not aligned to the strategic
  plan**; **permits outside consulting**; professional development is **not capped**.
- "Provides little to nothing in the way of accountability and looks more favorable to the individual... than
  the children of this district."

**Framing correction.** "The contract doesn't allow him to be fired" is **too strong** and would not survive
scrutiny (Iowa law + the board-discretion clause). The accurate, defensible version: **the contract built in
no accountability on results, and when results collapsed the board declined to use the authority it had.**
Attribute the "no performance measures" critique to the 2022 community email; attribute the renew-then-keep
facts to news. **Open question:** is ICCSD's contract genuinely unusual vs. other large Iowa districts (does
it lack performance language others include)? Not confirmed — see research job 6.

## Sub-plot 3 — The community warned the board, in writing, for years

This is the strongest and least legally fraught thread: dated, specific, board-addressed warnings that name
the exact failures that later detonated. Senders are redacted in the source emails; **quote substance, do
not name senders.**

- **June 14, 2022 (Email B).** Objects to the ACT purchase (overpaying, conflict of interest, no public
  discussion of need) and to signing the superintendent contract with no accountability measures.
- **October 21, 2024 (Email C).** Asks for a **public SAVE budget and projections to 2050**; warns the
  district is committing capital to "wants not needs" with no long-term plan, and that **schools could be
  forced to close** if SAVE is drained now. Notes the **FY2023 audit was still not back** (FY2022's was
  filed by Feb 15, 2023).
- **June 10, 2025 (Email A).** "**Where is ICCSD's FY2023 Audit, which has been pulled from the agenda
  again?**" Invokes **fiduciary duty** by name, asks the board to read the audit **before** voting tens of
  millions on field houses, and flags a **$377,628.90 federal tax lien** (released) — a corroborating data
  point for the late-tax-filing / IRS-penalty thread.

**How to frame.** A short "they were warned" section, woven against the timeline: each warning lands months
to years before the matching failure. The community used the words ("fiduciary duty," "due diligence") that
the State Board of Education would later use in June 2026. The board cannot claim ignorance.

**The $377,628.90 federal tax lien** corroborates the news-file thread that the district incurred **$525,110
in IRS penalties (Sept 2023–June 2025)** for late payroll/excise filings. Worth confirming the lien via the
county recorder / federal records (job 6 can fold this in).
