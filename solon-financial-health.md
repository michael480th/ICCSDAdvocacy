# Solon Community School District — Financial Health Report

**Prepared:** June 2026
**Primary sources:** Solon Community School District **audited financial statements** for the
fiscal years ended **June 30, 2023** and **June 30, 2025** (independent auditor: Kay L. Chapman,
CPA PC, Muscatine, Iowa). Figures labeled *computed* are derived from those statements using the
formulas shown; all other figures are taken directly from the audits.

> **Bottom line: Solon CSD is in strong financial health.** It carries a robust liquidity cushion
> (~140 days of cash in the General Fund), reserves above its own Board policy target, back-to-back
> clean (unmodified) audit opinions, and a disciplined, ratio-driven budgeting process — all while
> absorbing rapid enrollment growth and a voter-approved building program without raising its tax
> rate.

## 2. Liquidity — the strongest part of the picture

- The General Fund held **$6.68M in cash and investments** at June 30, 2025, up from **$6.23M** at
  June 30, 2023.
- That equates to roughly **140 days of operating cash** (FY2025) — a thick cushion. As a frame of
  reference, municipal credit analysts generally treat ~60 days as healthy and flag districts that
  dip toward 30; Solon sits at more than double the comfortable line.
- Liquidity rose even as the district **intentionally drew down** a portion of its accumulated
  General Fund balance — i.e., it is spending from a position of strength, not stress.

## 3. Reserves & fund balance

- **General Fund balance trend:** $4.51M (FY2023) → $4.24M (FY2024) → **$3.95M (FY2025)** — a
  controlled ~$0.56M draw over two years, consistent with management's stated plan to spend down
  some pandemic-era surplus while keeping ratios above target.
- **Solvency ratio** drifted from ~22.6% to ~19.1% over the same span but remains **comfortably
  above the Board's 10–15% policy range.**
- **Total governmental fund balance was $24.97M at June 30, 2025** — but read this carefully: the
  bulk of the year-over-year jump is **~$12.7M of unspent general-obligation bond proceeds** held
  in the construction fund, plus restricted SAVE and debt-service money. The meaningful *operating*
  reserve is the **General Fund (~$3.95M / ~140 days)**, not the $24.97M headline.
- Net position of governmental activities stood at **$41.2M** (June 30, 2025).

## 4. Operating results

| ($ millions) | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| General Fund revenues | 17.11 | 16.88 | 17.15 |
| General Fund expenditures | 17.04 | 17.32 | ~17.46 |
| Net change in GF balance | +0.05 | −0.27 | −0.29 |

Revenues are stable; the small FY2024–FY2025 deficits are **deliberate draws on reserve**, not
revenue shortfalls. Salary and benefits have stayed within the Board's 75–80% of-General-Fund
range, and a **5-year teacher agreement (3.25%/yr)** gives multi-year cost certainty.

## 5. Growth & capital program

- Solon is a **fast-growing district** (~1,450 students and rising on new housing development).
- Voters approved a **$25.5M general-obligation bond (March 2023, ~65% yes)** for elementary and
  intermediate expansion plus an indoor activity center, with construction in 2024–2025 — funded
  **without a tax-rate increase** (levy held around $16.2–16.3 per $1,000). Financing a multi-year
  build-out while holding the levy flat reflects sound debt/levy planning in a growth district.
- The ~$12.7M of bond proceeds still in the construction fund at June 30, 2025 reflects work in
  progress, not idle surplus.

## 6. Financial management rigor

The audits show a disciplined, controls-oriented operation:
- **Formal ratio governance:** the Board reviews solvency, percent-of-revenue-spent, and
  salary/benefits-as-%-of-GF **monthly and annually** against explicit target ranges.
- **Clean statutory compliance both years:** budget not exceeded, no questionable expenditures, no
  improper related-party transactions, no enrollment/weighting variances, and the **Certified
  Annual Report certified on time to the state.**
- **Unmodified audit opinions** in both FY2023 and FY2025.

## 7. Watch items (normal, not alarming)

- **Single recurring audit finding — "segregation of duties"** — flagged as a *material weakness*.
  This is the **near-universal finding for small district offices** (too few staff to fully split
  duties); management's response is the standard, accepted one. It is a structural artifact of
  size, not evidence of mismanagement.
- **Gradual reserve drawdown / solvency drift** (22.6% → 19.1%): worth monitoring, but still above
  policy and from a strong base.
- **Growth dependence:** continued enrollment growth and the associated capital/debt load are the
  main forward risks; current reserves and flat-levy financing mitigate them.
- **GASB-68 pension accounting** produced a negative *unrestricted* net position in governmental
  activities (e.g., −$230,929 in FY2023) — an accounting artifact common to virtually all Iowa
  districts, not an operating deficit.

---

## 8. Overall assessment

On the audited record, **Solon Community School District is financially healthy and well-managed**:
strong liquidity, reserves above its own policy floor, clean opinions, disciplined ratio-based
budgeting, and a growth/capital program delivered without raising the tax rate. The only audit
finding is the boilerplate small-district segregation-of-duties note, and the only genuine watch
items are the gentle reserve drawdown and the ordinary risks of being a fast-growing district —
both managed from a position of strength.
